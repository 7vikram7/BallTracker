# Is live line calling with phones worth pursuing? A study after four field sessions (2026-11-22)

For the project owner. Based on the recorded sessions of 2026-11-19, 11-20, 11-21 and 11-22
(decision log entries 18, 23, 24 and 30), replays of them with the current code, and the public
state of commercial line calling in November 2026.

## 1. Short answer

**Yes, continue, but change what the sessions are for.**
* Four sessions solved most of the *engineering*: the Mac keeps up, the clocks agree, calibration
  is automatic and follows the cameras when they move, and dead balls are no longer called.
* None of them measured *accuracy*: there has never been ground truth on a full court. So nobody,
  including me, can say how often the 25 OUTs of today were right, or how many real OUTs were
  missed.
* The next sessions should be measurements, not data gathering: 4 good cameras, firm mounts and a
  person logging the real calls.
* A go/no-go after three such sessions (section 7) decides between a club installation and
  something less demanding (post-match review).

Substantial improvements, all in the plumbing. Two numbers have not improved enough:
* the far-half disagreement (30-50 cm);
* the share of bounces seen by two cameras (35%).
Both depend on how many good cameras there are and where they stand, not on code.

## 3. What limits the calls now

**Accuracy depends on distance.** A camera measures a bounce well when it is near the line and low:
* 0.7 cm against chalk marks at close range (entry 7);
* 4-10 cm between two cameras on the half both see from nearby;
* 30-50 cm on the far half of a camera 13-17 m away.

So every line, along its whole length, needs a good camera within about 8 m. Two good cameras at
opposite corners cover only their own halves well. Four good cameras at the four corners are the
minimum; six (adding the middle of each sideline) would give two near views of most lines.

**Good cameras miss many bounces.** The iPhone and the Samsung each caught only 55-85% of the
rally bounces the other one reported. Some of the gap is false bounces, but much is real misses:
players in the way, the far half, 58 fps instead of 120 on the Samsung.

This is also why the last minutes were quiet:
* from minute 54 the Samsung caught only 53% of the iPhone's rally bounces, against 62-80% before;
* 7 of the session's 13 vetoed in-rally OUTs seen by one camera came in the last 7 minutes. In 5
  of them the iPhone saw the OUT and the Samsung, a good camera that could see the spot, reported
  nothing; in 2 it was the other way round;
* the server itself kept working: same bounce and call rate as before, the Mac at most 51% busy,
  no frames lost.

The Samsung's slip has two likely causes: the low morning sun on its end, and its calibration
creeping up to 2.3 px (the largest of the session). Its battery was also at 32%.

The veto rule is right: with one other camera that catches half the bounces, its silence makes a
lone OUT only 2-in-3 likely to be real. The fix is more good cameras:
* with three other cameras at 70% each, at least one confirms a real bounce 97% of the time;
* a lone camera's OUT then rarely has to be spoken or vetoed.

**Weak phones don't add up.**
* The Redmi 8A reported **0 bounces in 33 minutes**, including 5 minutes with a good calibration
  (1.0 px). It ran at 19 fps, took 46 ms per frame, and had a ball candidate in only 1-4% of
  frames.
* The Nothing reported 139 bounces. It caught 10-14% of bounces on its own half, and from minute
  30 it slowed to 16-18 fps (heat).
* Neither is worth a mount. A camera has to deliver at least 60 fps at 1080p to app code.

**Phones move.** Drift tracking compares each camera's picture every 10 s with its calibration
picture and updates the calibration automatically: 43 times today (iPhone 20, Samsung 19,
Nothing 4). It follows a phone that tilts or pans on its mount. It can't follow:
* a jump of 80-220 px (the Redmi) or a busy, changing view: it then warns "recalibrate";
* a phone that slides: a small residual error is left.

Firm mounts remove the problem at the source.

**Setup takes too long.** It took 18-34 minutes before calls could start, which leaves 30-45
minutes of play in a one-hour booking.

**Phones get hot and run down.** The iPhone was in "serious" thermal state from minute 20. The
Samsung's battery went from 63% to 32% in an hour.

## 4. Today's specific questions

**Did the server get overwhelmed in the last 10-15 minutes?** No.
* The Mac was at most 51% busy, no camera had frames deferred or dropped, and bounce, decision
  and call rates were the same as before.
* What changed was the Samsung catching fewer bounces (section 3).
* A "reset pipeline" button would not have helped, because the cause was a camera, not the server.
  So I haven't built one; if the server ever does fall behind, the dashboard shows it (deferred or
  dropped frames on the camera card), and that's when a reset would earn its place.
* There is one thing the logs can't show: whether the dashboard on the Mac actually spoke the 8
  OUTs it sent in that window. From the next session every phone also says the calls.

**Why was the Redmi recalibration ignored?**
* The recalibration at 39 minutes used 5 points and fitted at 3.6 px. Calibrations rougher than
  3 px are not used for calls, so that the error of a rough one doesn't decide a call.
* The snap at 41½ minutes fitted the lines well but was never confirmed with **Calibrate**.
* The Redmi detected no bounces even while its earlier 1.0 px calibration was in use, so it
  wouldn't have helped. Stop using it.

**Are you right that four good cameras are a must?** Yes (section 3). Four good cameras at the
corners is the minimum for both accuracy (a near camera for every line) and confirmation (a second
camera for most bounces). Weak extras don't substitute for a good one.

## 5. The competition (public information, November 2026)

* **Pro tours:** PlayReplay and Owl AI provide automated line calling for the PPA Tour and Major
  League Pickleball in 2026. PlayReplay works with two to four net-post cameras, raised
  $12 million, and Pickleball Inc. is its exclusive distributor for pickleball, down to
  recreational clubs. One unconfirmed report puts the club price at $3.5-4.5k per court per year.
* **Consumer:** SwingVision calls lines from one iPhone, for tennis and pickleball. Its tennis
  figure is 97% correct within 10 cm of the line (the company's figure; no independent pickleball
  test found). A two-camera version was planned.
* **Cheap devices:** In/Out, a single net-mounted device (about $275).

**What that means.**
* A setup of borrowed phones that takes 20-30 minutes won't beat a one-phone app on convenience.
* The niche left for this project:
  * a low-cost *permanent* installation at one club (fixed cameras, calibrated once, a small
    computer);
  * a self-hosted system the club owns;
  * an excellent learning project and a base for the analytics in the original brief (players,
    stats, coaching), where the line calls are only the first layer.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Measure every session from now on.** This is the most important change.
   * Have a person tap a "real call" button (OUT / IN / let) on a phone at each point. The
     server timestamps the taps, and the report then shows recall (real OUTs called) and false
     OUTs.
   * Plus, once per session, 10 chalk-marked bounces near the far lines, to settle the far-half
     bias. `scripts/score_marks.py` exists.
   * I can build the tap logger (a web page; no app install).
2. **Four good cameras at the four corners**, about 2-2.5 m high and 1-1.5 m outside the court,
   each seeing its near corner with margin:
   * the iPhone 17 and the Samsung;
   * the iPhone 16 (it has the app);
   * one more good phone (a used iPhone 12 or later delivers 120 fps).
   * Retire the Redmi; keep the Nothing only as a spare.
3. **Firm mounts:** fence clamps or tripods weighted with sandbags, and the same marked spots every
   time. Saved calibrations then fit at connection, and setup drops towards 10 minutes.
4. **Power and heat:** a battery pack for every phone, and shade for the iPhone.
5. **One network:** the router again, phones on Wi-Fi only.
6. **Install the new apps:** calls spoken on every phone, Stop session.
7. **Don't add features until the measurements say what's wrong.** The next code changes should
   come from the scored sessions: which lines and which cameras get calls wrong or miss them.

## 7. Go / no-go

After three measured sessions with four good cameras:

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Clear OUTs (more than 10 cm out) called | at least 90% |
| Wrong OUTs spoken | at most 1 per hour |
| Bounces within 10 cm of a line | right or silent ("unsure") 95% of the time |
| Bounce → call | under 1 s |
| Setup | under 10 minutes |

* **Targets met:** go for a fixed installation at one court (fixed cameras or old iPhones
  permanently mounted, a small always-on computer), then players and stats.
* **Accuracy fine but setup and convenience not:** the phones are the problem, not the method.
  Same decision, fixed hardware.
* **Accuracy not met with four good cameras:** stop live calling. Use the same pipeline for
  post-match review: no latency limit, heavier processing, and reviewing disputed calls from the
  recording. That is a smaller promise but still useful.
